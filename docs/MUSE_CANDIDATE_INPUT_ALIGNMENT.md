# Frozen 7,500-update candidate: pre-action alignment gate

The requested 200-action training-initialization test was stopped **before
the first action** because live inputs did not match the recorded training
inputs. No rollout success or failure claim can be made. The frozen checkpoint
SHA-256 is `62424f27c3e94bf79a1cf039290b9f3d6d4336b9a7aec301bc92eadcc2bade3e`;
the checkpoint was not altered.

The original saved simulator state and frame-0 RGB hashes matched the earlier
training initialization and recorded training frame. Live SAM measurements,
validity, and SAM-history tensors matched the stored training measurements
exactly. Two other inputs failed the gate:

* The live raw robot vector differed from the recorded training vector by up
  to 0.00991 in its source units; the largest normalized difference was
  0.3369, on a gripper channel. The original HDF5 observation equals the
  canonical training robot vector exactly, while a second simulator
  observation after forcing observable refresh matched the live vector.
* The A100 live NF4 Muse feature hash differed from the H200 training feature
  hash. SAM and model constructors had enabled TF32, while training explicitly
  disabled it. Resetting TF32 on A100 did not make its feature bits equal to
  H200; a no-action A100 probe reported mean absolute feature difference 0.3345,
  max 66.0, cosine 0.9854, with identical token IDs and attention masks. This
  comparison changes both hardware and runtime image, so it does not isolate
  hardware as the sole cause.

## Action-free robot-state timing sweep

For all 150 saved simulator states, the robot observation was regenerated by
`set_init_state`, without stepping any actions, and compared against recorded
HDF5 robot observations at offsets -3 through +3. For all 149 comparable
frames, **the joint positions and gripper positions regenerated from
`states[t]` match recorded robot observation `t-1` exactly**. EEF channels at
that offset have mean absolute difference about 0.000177. The training RGB
rendering code renders RGB frame `t` from `states[t]`, while its robot vector
is copied from recorded HDF5 observation `t`. This establishes a one-frame
robot-observation mismatch in this episode; it does not alone prove which
acquisition stage caused the source timing or quantify its effect on control.
The original action replay shows that source actions can reproduce the task,
but did not validate the robot-observation timing used by training.

## Action-free Muse parity probe

An H200 probe with the same NF4 checkpoint, RGB, processor, and disabled TF32
reproduced the saved training feature hash exactly. A second probe loaded and
ran the same live SAM initialization and replay path, then restored the
training TF32 flags and disabled autocast for Muse; on H200, its first-frame
feature bits, token IDs and mask also matched that reference exactly. The
frozen-candidate runner now uses H200 for the Muse/SAM worker and restores
those precision flags immediately before each Muse forward. This repair was
tested on the initial feature in an action-free probe, not through an entire
rollout.

## Corrected data artifact and remaining blocker

`/libero/muse_candidate_aligned_episode_v1/episode_0000.npz` is a **separate,
untrained diagnostic artifact** in which the robot vector at each frame is
regenerated from `states[t]`; the original actions, SAM features, timestamps
and stored RGB remain unchanged. The 512-pixel rendered RGB corresponding
to `states[t]` remains in the original render directory. The aligned first
robot vector matches the live vector hash. This is not a complete aligned
multi-episode split and its normalization statistics have not been refitted.

The frozen 7,500-update checkpoint was trained using the original misaligned
robot observations. A corrected episode does not retroactively repair that
checkpoint's training history. Feeding the future recorded training vector to
the simulator policy, or weakening the gate, would conceal rather than fix the
issue. A valid next learning experiment requires an explicitly chosen aligned
dataset/normalization and a newly trained or continued checkpoint; it would no
longer be the exact frozen 7,500-update checkpoint requested for this run.

## Aligned build, retrain and gated rollout

`/libero/muse_aligned_development_v1` is a complete aligned copy of the
development dataset: all 42 episodes have their robot observation regenerated
from the source simulator states, while RGB, SAM measurements, actions,
timestamps and split assignment are unchanged. Source fingerprint
`b7bae449…`, new fingerprint `86d9fdf0…`, initial robot hash
`04d32a65…` (matching the live gate), mean absolute robot change `0.0057`,
maximum `0.0476`. No actions were executed during construction. Verification
also confirmed the episode-0 robot vectors equal the audited timing sweep.

A fresh query-normalized full-episode fit used the aligned dataset for 7,500
updates (H200, batch 1, seed 7, AdamW lr 1e-4). The saved checkpoint SHA-256 is
`0cbefd66c6345ec4b4ae465fcc1a1d1ad9c2874800813921e201dd9fe8948e68`, stats
SHA-256 `e32acff4…`; the eight-frame in-sample first-action MAE fell from
1.0024 to 0.2556 (normalized). This is a new checkpoint, not the frozen 7,500
checkpoint.

The gated 200-action rollout from `episode_0000` then passed **every** initial
parity check exactly: initial state `02ffa3fe…`, RGB `6cffc8a3…`, Muse feature
`c0edda8a…`, robot raw vector `04d32a65…`, SAM measurements, validity, and all
conditioning tensors. The policy executed 200 actions without success (the
episode ends at 200 actions). Trace: 19 clipped gripper channels, sampled
gripper range `-1.51…1.16`.

Failure analysis (`mask_trajectory.json`, saved masks and raw robot):

* End-effector position error against the recorded demonstration exceeded
  3 cm by step 15, 18.7 cm at the demo's grasp step 47, and 23.7 cm at step 90.
* Gripper command sign agreed with the demonstration on only 49% of steps
  0–149 and **23% of the grasp window 47–141**; three consecutive
  disagreements begin at step 50 and commands flap throughout.
* The milk's upward image-centroid shift after step 40 was `0.0024` versus
  `0.0163` in the recorded demonstration; its visible area never fell below
  68% of the matched recorded frame. The milk was never lifted.
* Video and masks show the arm reaching the milk area but never securing it;
  the arm retreats while the milk remains on the table.

Earliest break: **approach and grasp**, not lift or placement. The aligned
policy reaches the neighbourhood but diverges from the demonstrated approach,
fails to hold a grasp at the demonstrated close time, and therefore never
lifts the milk. This also matches the earlier native and pre-alignment
rollouts, so the one-frame robot misalignment was a real input bug but was not
the only cause of open-loop failure.

The requested two additional sampling seeds were conditional on success and
were not run. Modal credits were nearly exhausted after the gated rollout
(~$0.8 remaining), so no further GPU experiment was started. The aligned
checkpoint is a resumable starting point for the next experiment; test
initializations remain untouched.
